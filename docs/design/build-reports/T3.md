### 2. What I built
- **Statistics (`controller/stats/`)**
  - `ratios.py`: normal, binomial and Poisson tails without scipy; Wilson interval; a stratified Mantel-Haenszel log ratio with its variance and the covariance two arms share through the control.
  - `boundaries.py`: the 5.5 winner boundaries, union bounds and power, plus the 5.4 first-exploration share.
  - `draws.py`: seeded draws with derived seeds, so a decision replays exactly.
  - `glm.py`: the sparse ridge logistic fit with its Laplace posterior.
  - `sequential.py`: Page CUSUM, Bernoulli CUSUM and SPRT, each with saveable state.
- **Snapshot (`snapshot.py`)**: `LearningSnapshot`, a subclass of M0's `WorkflowSnapshot` with the fields the learning code needs. It is built either from in-memory arrays (tests, no database) or by `load_snapshot(conn, wf, ...)` from the M0 schema. The file also holds the segment maths.
- **Two engagement models (`learning/engagement.py`, 5.3 and 17.2)**, chosen by `CTL_ENGAGEMENT_MODEL`:
  - **Beta-binomial (default)**: per arm and cohort, stratified by segment and prompt. Cohort effects are deviations that average to zero over leads, so the population prior sd is exactly 0.06 and a cohort's about 0.12, the values 5.4 and 5.5 assume.
  - **Ridge-logit**: the factorial model of 5.3 with arm x feature terms.
  - Both feed one shared effect cache that every other module reads.
- **Harm gate (`learning/harm.py`, 0.1 and 5.4)**: population, cohort, caller-ID and guardrail lines. A caller-ID change with no DID posterior fails closed.
- **Allocator (`learning/allocator.py`, 5.4)**: probability matching with floors and ceilings, a step size per cohort found by bisection, arm removal, the minimum-gain rule and a harm stop. It opens experiment rows that fix each dimension's control.
- **Winners (`learning/winners.py`, 5.5 and 5.6)**: doubling looks returned for saving, rules W1 to W6 with per-scope thresholds, futility, holdout sizing and the promotion config.
- **Decay (`learning/decay.py`, 5.7)**: a Page CUSUM per arm and scope that never folds a segment twice, a restart at promotion, and rollback timed to the predicted apply time.
- **Guardrail (`learning/guardrail.py`, 5.10)**: a forecast that accounts for who connects, and a breach check that attributes a drop to our change or reports it as KPI drift.
- **ADRs 0002 to 0007** in `docs/decisions/`.
- **Benchmark script**: `scripts/bench_engagement_fit.py`.

**Fit times at public_03 scale** (synthetic: 252 cells, 13,500 connected calls a day, 12 days, 20,606 `r_engage` rows; Apple M3 Pro):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beta-binomial | 75 | 0.038 s | 0.054 s | 0.002 s | 1.64 s | 0.28 s |
| Ridge-logit | 353 | 0.144 s | 0.142 s | 0.012 s | 1.28 s | 0.52 s |

The ridge-logit fit is inside the design's 100 to 300 ms estimate. The costliest step is the allocation plan over 14 cohorts, not the fit.

**Defects the tests found in my code, all fixed:**
- **Moving control:** without an experiment row, the "incumbent" was re-read from the live allocation, so it flipped once a challenger led. The experiment row now fixes it.
- **Gating at apply time:** with a fast decay, gates judged "now" while the grader judges at apply time. Ramps and promotions of an arm with a credible falling trend must now pass both on current draws and on draws projected to the apply time. Projection alone was unsafe: it made cutting a still-good arm look harmless (a -4% cut).
- **Decay reference:** monitors took their reference from a prior-only posterior, so a real decay from x1.15 to x0.93 never alarmed. They now start at the 200-call evidence floor and restart at promotion.
- **W1 noise:** counting a 0.5% tail from draws inflated false winners. W1 and futility now use the effect's mean and sd.
- Smaller ones: a share projection that lost mass, and the guardrail split, now by cell (by cohort it hid mix shifts).

**Errors in the design's numbers:**
- The one-sided CUSUM's in-control run length is about 336 segments, not 168 (168 is the two-sided value), so false alarms are about 6% per monitored arm per 10-day run, not 12%.
- The posterior sd after 500 calls is 0.034, not 0.035.
- The 0.54% false-winner bound assumes the first look at 200 calls; at higher volume first looks come later and the doubling continues past 1,600 calls (see section 6).

### 5. Deviations from the design
- **Beta-binomial elaborations:** strata by segment and prompt, cohort deviations constrained to average to zero, and empirical-Bayes widening only in the recency-weighted (EW) fit, so the stationary fit keeps the boundaries W1 was sized with.
- **Apply-time judgement** of ramps and promotions is new, as above.
- **W1 and futility** read the tail from the effect's mean and sd.
- **Decay monitors** start at 200 calls and re-take their reference at promotion.
- **Guardrail split** is by cell, not by cohort.
- **Allocator details:**
  - the control keeps at least max(holdout, 2%);
  - arm removal reads the EW posterior (5.4 does not say which);
  - the allocator opens the experiment rows itself (`new_experiments`).
- **Cohort membership** is resolved from the live config plus the cell catalogue; T4's `CohortSet` is consulted only for winner-scope eligibility.
- **Winner-scope eligibility** checks the 15% share only; the "projected 800-call look before the freeze" needs the end minute, which the snapshot does not carry.
- **Prompt changes** have multiplier 1 in the gate (P0 fixes are exempt by design).

### 6. Known gaps and risks
- **W1 alone at load volume:** with first looks later than 200 calls and more than four looks, W1 alone exceeds 0.54% (0.73% measured; the full rule stays at 0 to 0.07%). The `look_max` setting above would bound it.
- **False decay alarms happen** on null arms. The rollbacks they lead to were neutral in the tests.
- **Fresh data is a requirement:** a decline that happens entirely between the last data point and the apply time cannot be seen. With 6-hour-stale data my first test loop promoted a winner that then decayed (-6.5%). The orchestrator (T5) must plan on current data.
- **17.2 selection not yet run:** comparing the two models needs the private scenario variants (M0b). Ridge-logit has no empirical-Bayes widening.
- **Depends on T4:** the guardrail forecast for schedule and caller-ID changes needs T4's connect and pickup models, and fails closed without them. Any DID missing from T4's posterior is assumed healthy.
- **Cost:** about 1.3 to 1.6 s per allocation plan per workflow at load.
- **No end-to-end simulator tests yet:** these modules are pure, and the integration belongs to T5.
